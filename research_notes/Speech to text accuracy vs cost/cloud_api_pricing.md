# Commercial Speech-to-Text API Pricing, Streaming, and Data Handling (October 2026)

Scope: ElevenLabs Scribe, Speechmatics, AssemblyAI, Deepgram, OpenAI, Google Cloud Speech-to-Text, AWS Transcribe, Azure AI Speech, Rev.ai, Gladia, Soniox, Mistral Voxtral, plus Groq/Fireworks-hosted Whisper as notable low-cost entrants. All research conducted via web search/fetch in October 2026; access date for all sources below is 2026-10-08 unless otherwise noted. Prices normalized to USD/audio-hour where the source gave per-minute or per-second rates (1 hr = 60 min). Where official vendor pricing pages returned placeholder/JS-rendered values that could not be confirmed, this is noted explicitly and the most recent third-party-reported figure is given instead, flagged as such.

## What is the list price per hour for batch vs. streaming, and what are the add-on/volume-discount terms?

### Takeaway
Pure batch rates on commodity models now range from under $0.05/hr (Groq-hosted Whisper, Fireworks Whisper) to ~$1.44/hr entry-tier (AWS Transcribe Standard); most specialized STT vendors (Deepgram, AssemblyAI, ElevenLabs) cluster around $0.15–$0.25/hr batch and $0.30–$0.45/hr real-time, while enterprise-oriented Speechmatics and Azure run higher ($0.45–$0.80/hr) unless discounted. Nearly every vendor charges a premium for streaming over batch, and per-feature add-ons (diarization, PII redaction, entity detection, keyterm/custom-vocabulary boosting) are billed separately and stack additively on top of the base rate.

### Inferences
- The market has bifurcated into (a) commodity/open-weight Whisper hosting (Groq, Fireworks) competing almost entirely on price, at $0.04–$0.09/hr batch, with no differentiated accuracy/latency claims, and (b) specialized ASR vendors (Deepgram, AssemblyAI, Speechmatics, ElevenLabs, Soniox, Gladia) competing on streaming latency and accuracy while charging a 1.5–3x premium over batch for real-time.
- Big-cloud generalist platforms (AWS, Azure, Google) are now the **most expensive** per-hour options at list/entry-tier pricing, which likely explains why Microsoft is aggressively discounting its new MAI-Transcribe-2 model ($0.10/hr promotional) to compete with the specialized vendors rather than keeping legacy Azure Speech pricing.
- Add-on fees (diarization, PII redaction, keyterm prompting) are priced similarly in absolute terms (~$0.02–$0.15/hr) across AssemblyAI, Deepgram, and Speechmatics, suggesting these are becoming commoditized features rather than differentiators — the differentiation is in base transcription quality/latency.

## Which providers support real-time streaming, and what latency do they claim?

### Takeaway
Nearly all providers in scope now offer real-time streaming except AWS Transcribe's accuracy/latency claims are notably absent and Groq's Whisper hosting is batch-only; stated latency claims cluster in the 150–300ms range for the fastest specialized vendors (ElevenLabs, Deepgram, AssemblyAI, Mistral Voxtral Realtime), while Speechmatics and AWS trade latency for accuracy at ~500ms+ by default.

### Cited Findings
- **ElevenLabs Scribe v2 Realtime**: vendor-claimed ~150ms latency — [elevenlabs.io/docs/capabilities/speech-to-text](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/capabilities/speech-to-text) (fetched 2026-10-08).
- **Speechmatics**: configurable `max_delay` parameter from 0.7–4 seconds; default of 4s "maximizes accuracy," 1.5s is the vendor-recommended starting point for voice-agent use cases — [speechmatics.com latency article](https://www.speechmatics.com/company/articles-and-news/what-is-latency-in-speech-recognition-systems). An independent (non-vendor) benchmark reports Speechmatics' median Time-to-Final-Segment at 495ms (Pipecat benchmark, April 2026) — [referenced in search results, original Pipecat benchmark not directly fetched].
- **AssemblyAI Universal-Streaming**: vendor markets "sub-300ms" time-to-complete with neural end-of-turn detection around 300ms; a separate description claims "sub-200ms end-to-end latency" for the WebSocket streaming layer — these two vendor-adjacent claims are not fully consistent and should be treated as approximate marketing figures rather than a single hard number — [search summary citing assemblyai.com/docs/streaming/benchmarks](https://assemblyai.com/docs/streaming/benchmarks).
- **Deepgram**: vendor claims "sub-300ms end-to-end latency" for Nova-3/Flux streaming — [search summary, 2026]. Deepgram also publishes a dedicated latency-measurement guide for customers — [developers.deepgram.com/docs/measuring-streaming-latency](https://developers.deepgram.com/docs/measuring-streaming-latency.mdx).
- **OpenAI Realtime API**: no specific official transcription-latency figure was found in this research pass — **gap**.
- **Google Cloud (Chirp 3)**: supports real-time streaming in addition to batch, with data-residency options; no specific official latency figure was found — **gap** (the per-minute pricing figure found, $0.016/min, came from a third-party aggregator, not an official Google latency/performance page) — [OpenRouter Chirp 3 listing](https://openrouter.ai/models/google/chirp-3).
- **AWS Transcribe**: streaming is supported at the same list price as batch, but AWS does not appear to publish an official end-to-end latency number; practitioner reports place it in the 500ms–1.5s range, well behind Deepgram's published ~200–300ms figure — [search summary, "AWS Transcribe vs Deepgram latency," 2026] — this is a practitioner/third-party observation, not a vendor claim, and should be labeled as such.
- **Azure AI Speech**: offers Real-time Transcription, Fast Transcription, and Batch Transcription tiers; no specific official latency figure for Real-time Transcription was found in this pass — **gap**.
- **Rev.ai**: supports both streaming and async transcription through the same API; no specific official latency figure found in search results — **gap**.
- **Gladia**: vendor claims "less than 300 milliseconds" latency for real-time transcription, positioned for voice AI agents and contact centers — [gladia.io blog, cited in search summary](https://www.gladia.io/blog/getting-started-with-gladia-how-to-build-with-our-stt-api-features).
- **Soniox**: offers real-time streaming (separate SKU from async, see pricing section); no specific official latency figure found in this research pass — **gap**.
- **Mistral Voxtral Realtime**: vendor claims sub-200ms latency with a configurable delay range of 80ms–2400ms; default "sweet spot" of 480ms balances latency and accuracy, with only 1–2 additional WER percentage points versus the 2.4-second/near-offline setting — [gend.co Voxtral Transcribe 2 summary](https://www.gend.co/blog/voxtral-transcribe-2); official docs at [docs.mistral.ai/capabilities/audio_transcription](https://docs.mistral.ai/capabilities/audio_transcription).
- **Groq-hosted Whisper**: batch/async only; no first-party real-time streaming endpoint identified — [search summary, 2026] (see pricing section for detail).
- **Fireworks-hosted Whisper**: no information on real-time streaming support was found in this research pass — **gap**.

### Inferences
- Vendors that publish a specific, low (sub-300ms) latency number (ElevenLabs, Deepgram, AssemblyAI, Gladia, Mistral Voxtral Realtime) are explicitly targeting the voice-agent/conversational-AI market; vendors without a published number (AWS, Azure, Rev.ai, Soniox, Google, OpenAI) are either not prioritizing that use case or simply have not made the figure public-facing.
- Nearly every vendor's latency claim is self-reported (vendor benchmark) rather than independently verified; the one clearly independent figure found (Speechmatics' 495ms median TTFS from the Pipecat benchmark) suggests real-world latency can run noticeably higher than the lowest marketing numbers (e.g., ElevenLabs' 150ms, Mistral's 480ms default) — none of these are apples-to-apples since methodology differs.

### Gaps
- No official latency figures found for OpenAI Realtime API, Google Cloud Speech-to-Text, AWS Transcribe, Azure AI Speech, Rev.ai, Soniox, or Fireworks. These would need direct vendor-doc review (e.g., Azure's "Fast Transcription" docs, AWS Transcribe streaming docs) to close.
- No independently (non-vendor) verified latency benchmark was found for Deepgram, AssemblyAI, ElevenLabs, Gladia, or Mistral — all figures above are vendor-reported and should be labeled as such in the final report.

## Which providers support custom vocabulary / keyterm prompting?

### Takeaway
Custom vocabulary / keyterm boosting is now close to table-stakes — every major vendor researched offers some mechanism (keyterm lists, phrase hints, custom vocabulary files, or in-context prompting), but the mechanisms, term-count limits, and pricing differ significantly, and OpenAI's models notably lack a dedicated keyterm-boosting feature equivalent to the others.

### Cited Findings
- **ElevenLabs**: Keyterm prompting supports up to 1,000 terms for batch (Scribe v2); also offers entity detection across 65 types and diarization for up to 32 speakers — [elevenlabs.io/docs/capabilities/speech-to-text](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/capabilities/speech-to-text) (fetched 2026-10-08). Billed as a separate add-on at +$0.05/hr per third-party pricing tracker — [Perkstack](https://perkstack.co/blog/cheapest-elevenlabs-scribe-api).
- **AssemblyAI**: Keyterms prompting supports up to 1,000 terms (Universal-3.5 Pro async) or 100–200 terms depending on the model; billed as an add-on (+$0.05/hr async, +$0.04/hr streaming English) — [assemblyai.com/pricing.md](https://www.assemblyai.com/pricing.md).
- **Deepgram**: Keyterm Prompting offered as a line-item add-on ($0.0013/min PAYG, $0.0012/min Growth) — [deepgram.com/pricing](https://deepgram.com/pricing) (fetched 2026-10-08). Nova-3 is marketed as the first STT model to combine real-time multilingual transcription with "live vocabulary injection" in one API call — [search summary, 2026].
- **AWS Transcribe**: Supports custom vocabularies (to improve recognition of specific terms) and separately, custom vocabulary filters (to mask/flag/delete words, e.g. profanity); vocabulary filters are language-specific and must match the media's language code — [AWS official docs: Creating a vocabulary filter](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/transcribe/latest/dg/vocabulary-filter-create.html) and [Using a custom vocabulary filter](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/transcribe/latest/dg/vocabulary-filter-using.html).
- **Azure AI Speech**: "Phrase lists" provide lightweight, no-training-required runtime customization — add participant names, product names, and domain jargon to boost recognition without retraining a model — [Microsoft devblogs, Azure Speech-to-Text optimization, Oct 31 2025](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/ise/azure-speech-to-text-optimization/). Azure also separately supports full Custom Speech model training for deeper domain adaptation (not confirmed with a specific citation in this pass).
- **Google Cloud Speech-to-Text**: "Speech adaptation" lets developers supply phrase hints/boosts, built-in class tokens (numbers, addresses, dates), and reusable PhraseSet/CustomClass resources for domain vocabulary — inline phrase hints are lightweight, while PhraseSet/CustomClass resources require more setup — [Google Cloud Speech V2 SpeechAdaptation docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ruby/docs/reference/google-cloud-speech-v2/0.7.0/Google-Cloud-Speech-V2-SpeechAdaptation).
- **Speechmatics**: no explicit custom-vocabulary pricing or term-count limit was found in the official pricing page fetch; general vendor marketing references accuracy customization but without the concrete mechanism cited in this pass — **gap**.
- **Rev.ai**: Custom Vocabulary API available for both streaming and async modes; default limit of 150 custom-vocabulary requests per 2 minutes, up to 6,000 phrases per transcription job for English (1,000 for other languages) — [docs.rev.ai/api/custom-vocabulary](https://docs.rev.ai/api/custom-vocabulary/).
- **Gladia**: Offers custom vocabulary / "hot words" / keyword boosting, marketed as one of the highest-leverage accuracy levers, especially for repeated domain-specific terms (company names, technical jargon) — [gladia.io blog on custom vocabulary](https://www.gladia.io/blog/custom-vocabulary-for-contact-center-transcription-product-names-brands-and-agent-jargon). Specific term-count limits or add-on pricing not found — **gap**.
- **Soniox**: Supports "custom context" for domain-specific vocabulary adjustment, positioned as eliminating the need for separate fine-tuned models — included in the base real-time rate at no extra charge — [soniox.com docs, security-and-privacy page referencing capabilities](https://soniox.com/docs/stt/security-and-privacy).
- **OpenAI**: No dedicated keyterm/custom-vocabulary boosting feature equivalent to the above was found for whisper-1, gpt-4o-transcribe, or gpt-4o-mini-transcribe; the models instead accept a free-text "prompt" field that can bias output stylistically, but this is not the same mechanism as a structured keyterm/phrase-boost list — **flagged as a likely gap in OpenAI's offering relative to competitors**, though this absence (rather than presence) was inferred from the lack of any custom-vocabulary feature surfacing in searches, not from an explicit vendor statement that no such feature exists — treat with appropriate caution.
- **Mistral Voxtral**: no explicit custom-vocabulary/keyterm feature was found in this research pass — **gap**.

### Inferences
- For a product with VC/startup jargon and course-specific terminology (as implied by the requester's use case), AssemblyAI, Deepgram, ElevenLabs, Rev.ai, and Gladia all offer a structured keyterm/custom-vocabulary mechanism with reasonably generous term limits (100–6,000 depending on vendor), making them more directly suited to that need than OpenAI's prompt-based approach.
- Soniox's inclusion of "custom context" in the base real-time price (no separate add-on fee found) is notable compared to AssemblyAI/Deepgram/ElevenLabs, which all charge extra for keyterm prompting as a line item.

### Gaps
- No confirmed custom-vocabulary mechanism, term limits, or pricing for Speechmatics, Mistral Voxtral, Azure's Custom Speech (beyond phrase lists), or Fireworks/Groq-hosted Whisper.
- Could not confirm whether OpenAI's audio models support any structured keyterm boosting beyond free-text prompting — needs a direct check of platform.openai.com/docs for gpt-4o-transcribe/whisper-1.

## Data retention, training opt-out, zero-data-retention, and regional/compliance terms

### Takeaway
Policies vary widely: several vendors (Soniox, Fireworks, OpenAI API, Mistral paid tiers) now default to **not** training on customer data, with opt-in required; others (ElevenLabs non-enterprise, AWS Transcribe) default to allowing use of audio for service/model improvement unless the customer actively opts out; true zero-data-retention is generally an Enterprise-tier or negotiated feature everywhere it exists (ElevenLabs, OpenAI, Gladia, Groq, AssemblyAI streaming-with-opt-out). SOC2 Type 2 and GDPR compliance are now broadly standard among the specialized vendors (AssemblyAI, Deepgram, Soniox, Rev.ai) and the hyperscalers.

### Cited Findings
- **ElevenLabs**: On non-enterprise tiers, audio "can be used to improve ElevenLabs' models unless you proactively opt out," and the opt-out is **prospective only** (data already submitted may already be in training sets); it also only blocks model-training use, not security monitoring, analytics, or metadata handling. Zero Retention Mode (ZRM) is Enterprise-only, intended for healthcare/banking/sensitive-data customers; most request/response data is deleted immediately once the request completes, but ZRM applies to **API use only** (not the web UI/playground), and voice-cloning workflows are **excluded entirely** from ZRM. No GDPR or SOC2 certification was mentioned on the ZRM documentation page itself — [elevenlabs.io/docs/resources/zero-retention-mode](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/resources/zero-retention-mode) (fetched 2026-10-08).
- **Speechmatics**: Offers a 33%-off discount specifically for **opting in** to let audio improve its models (implying the default, non-discounted state is opt-out/no training) — [speechmatics.com/our-technology/pricing](https://www.speechmatics.com/our-technology/pricing) (fetched 2026-10-08). No explicit SOC2/GDPR/EU-hosting statement was captured from the pricing page itself in this pass — **gap, needs a trust-center page check**.
- **AssemblyAI**: For Streaming, opting out of model training yields **zero data retention of audio and transcripts** (some metadata is still stored for logging/billing). For async (pre-recorded), Final Transcription Artifacts are stored for 30 days by default and auto-deleted, with a configurable TTL. Training opt-out is forward-looking only (does not retroactively remove already-used data) — [assemblyai.com/docs/data-retention-and-model-training](https://www.assemblyai.com/docs/data-retention-and-model-training). SOC2 Type 1 **and** Type 2 certified; GDPR-aligned via Privacy Policy/DPA — [search summary of AssemblyAI security docs]. EU data residency is available via a dedicated EU endpoint (api.eu.assemblyai.com), guaranteeing data never leaves the EU; main EU endpoint runs on AWS eu-west-1 (Dublin), with EU streaming additionally available via AWS eu-north-1 (Stockholm) — [AssemblyAI EU data residency docs](https://www.assemblyai.com/docs/guides/how_to_use_the_eu_endpoint) and [endpoints-and-data-zones docs](https://www.assemblyai.com/docs/streaming/endpoints-and-data-zones.md).
- **Deepgram**: Model-training participation ("Model Improvement Partnership Program") appears to require an explicit opt-out via a `mip_opt_out=true` query parameter in one source, while another states users are by-default opted **out** of sharing audio and must actively opt in — these two descriptions are **inconsistent** and should be verified directly against developers.deepgram.com before being treated as settled — [developers.deepgram.com/docs/the-deepgram-model-improvement-partnership-program](https://developers.deepgram.com/docs/the-deepgram-model-improvement-partnership-program). Zero data retention is supported via a "no-storage configuration" when properly set up. Deepgram's infrastructure is stated to be SOC-2 Type 2 and GDPR compliant — [search summary, 2026].
- **OpenAI**: API inputs are **not** used for model training/service-improvement by default unless the customer opts in. Default API data retention is up to 30 days (for abuse monitoring), with stricter options available depending on need. Whisper audio-transcription endpoints can qualify for Zero Data Retention (ZDR) — data not retained even for the brief abuse-monitoring window — but this requires enterprise eligibility approval and is **not a self-serve toggle** (must be arranged with the OpenAI account team). OpenAI maintains SOC2 Type 2, GDPR, CCPA, and HIPAA (via BAA) compliance — [openai.com/api-data-privacy](https://openai.com/api-data-privacy) (summarized via search, official page not directly fetched in this pass — should be verified directly).
- **Google Cloud Speech-to-Text**: By default, the service does **not** log customer audio or transcripts; customers can opt in to a "data logging" program, where logged data is used only "to train and improve Google products and services" and explicitly **not** to target ads or other products. Notably, logged data (once a customer has opted in) is **not deleted when the project is deleted** — a retention nuance worth flagging for any privacy-sensitive buyer — [Google Cloud data-logging docs, cloud.google.com/speech-to-text/docs/data-logging](https://cloud.google.com/speech-to-text/docs/data-logging?hl=ja) (summarized via search). No specific SOC2/GDPR citation was captured for the Speech-to-Text service specifically in this pass, though Google Cloud as a platform broadly holds these certifications — **gap, service-specific confirmation not found**.

Amazon Transcribe **Medical** is explicitly exempted from this by default — [search summary citing AWS Transcribe service terms, 2026]. No AWS-Transcribe-specific SOC2/GDPR citation was captured in this pass, though these certifications are standard at the AWS platform level — **gap, service-specific confirmation not found**.
- **Azure AI Speech**: Stated to be SOC2, HIPAA, and GDPR compliant at a general level — [search summary citing a third-party compliance comparison article, not Microsoft's own compliance page directly](https://www.gladia.io/blog/azure-speech-services-vs-gladia-enterprise-sla-data-residency-compliance-comparison) — this is a secondhand characterization and should be confirmed against Microsoft's own Trust Center before being relied upon. No specific training-opt-out or data-retention-duration figure for Azure Speech was found — **gap**.
- **Rev.ai**: Undergoes an **annual SOC2 Type II attestation** by an independent third-party auditor. Designed to help customers comply with GDPR (Data Processing Addendum and Privacy Policy available). Also cites HIPAA, PCI, ISO 27001, FedRAMP, and CSA STAR Level 1 compliance. All customer data encrypted in transit and at rest — [rev.ai/security](https://www.rev.ai/security) and [rev.com/security](https://rev.com/security) (summarized via search). No explicit statement on whether audio is used for model training/improvement, or a zero-data-retention option, was found — **gap**.

By default, Gladia uses a France-based European provider to meet GDPR requirements, but can route to other geographies/providers on customer request — [docs.gladia.io/chapters/limits-and-specifications/data-retention](https://docs.gladia.io/chapters/limits-and-specifications/data-retention) and [gladia.io/security](https://gladia.io/security) (summarized via search). Operational logs contain only metadata (request IDs, timestamps, duration, latency, etc.) — explicitly **not** customer content, raw audio, or transcripts. Soniox states it is GDPR compliant, SOC 2 Type 2 compliant, ISO 27001:2022 compliant, and HIPAA compliant — [soniox.com/docs/stt/security-and-privacy](https://soniox.com/docs/stt/security-and-privacy) (summarized via search; strongest explicit no-training/no-default-storage stance found among all vendors researched).

Mistral is natively subject to GDPR and hosts data in the EU by default, prioritizing EU-based infrastructure providers — [search summary citing Mistral's commercial terms and data-retention articles, 2026] (official docs.mistral.ai privacy/legal pages not directly fetched in this pass — should be verified directly for precision).

### Inferences
- There is a clear split between vendors whose **default** posture is "no training without opt-in" (Soniox, Fireworks, OpenAI API, Mistral paid plans, Groq) versus vendors whose default is "may use for improvement unless you opt out" (ElevenLabs non-enterprise, AWS Transcribe, and ambiguously Deepgram, where two sources conflict). For a privacy-sensitive product (e.g., handling confidential VC/startup conversations), this distinction matters more than raw pricing and should be a hard filter, not just a comparison point.
- True zero-data-retention as a **self-serve, no-sales-call** option is rare — Soniox (no default storage) and Fireworks (ZDR as inference default) are the only two vendors researched where this appears to be the out-of-the-box behavior rather than an Enterprise-negotiated add-on.
- EU/GDPR-first hosting is explicit and prominent for Mistral (EU by default) and Gladia (France-based EU provider by default), which may matter if the product needs to represent itself as EU-data-resident without extra configuration.

### Gaps

## Free tier terms and vendor-reported English accuracy (WER) claims

### Takeaway
Free tiers range from zero (Soniox) to 50+ hours worth of credit (Gladia, Speechmatics); accuracy claims are universally vendor-reported and inconsistent in methodology (different test sets, different language/domain scope), so cross-vendor WER comparisons below should be treated as directional only, not precise rankings — this is explicitly flagged per-claim below.

### Cited Findings
- **ElevenLabs**: Free plan includes 10,000 credits/month (at 330 credits/minute for STT, this is roughly ~30 minutes/month of free speech-to-text) — [elevenlabs.io/pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing) (fetched 2026-10-08). Vendor-reported accuracy: 34 of 90+ supported languages achieve "excellent" accuracy (≤5% WER); others range from "high" to "moderate." Scribe v2 Medical reduces errors by 35% on clinical audio relative to the general model (**vendor-reported**) — [elevenlabs.io/docs/capabilities/speech-to-text](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/capabilities/speech-to-text).
- **Speechmatics**: $100 free signup credit, no card required (official page) — [speechmatics.com/our-technology/pricing](https://www.speechmatics.com/our-technology/pricing). Vendor-reported accuracy (Ursa model): 11.2% WER at lowest-latency setting vs. 10.25% WER in batch (an 8.5% relative degradation for real-time) — **vendor-reported**, [speechmatics.com best-in-class real-time ASR article](https://www.speechmatics.com/company/articles-and-news/best-in-class-real-time-asr-system). A separate, **independent** (non-vendor) Pipecat benchmark (April 2026) reported Speechmatics at 83.2% "perfect transcripts" and a 1.07% pooled WER on a voice-agent test set, the best accuracy in that particular benchmark — this one is independently sourced rather than vendor-reported, which is unusual among the figures collected here, though the original benchmark report itself was not directly fetched — [referenced via search summary].

### Inferences
- Accuracy marketing claims are not comparable across vendors as presented: each vendor benchmarks on its own test set (Deepgram explicitly on its "own internal test set," AssemblyAI and ElevenLabs on unspecified internal sets, Mistral on FLEURS, Speechmatics on its Ursa evaluation set). The one piece of cross-vendor independent evidence found (the "within 1–2 points on LibriSpeech/FLEURS" claim and the Open ASR Leaderboard figure for AssemblyAI) suggests that, in practice, top-tier vendors are much closer in raw clean-audio accuracy than their individual marketing numbers imply.
- Free-tier generosity does not correlate with base pricing: Gladia and Speechmatics, two of the more expensive vendors at list price, offer the largest free credits (€50 and $100 respectively), plausibly to offset their higher sticker price during evaluation.

### Gaps
