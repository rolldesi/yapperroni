# ASR Robustness: Accents, Noise, Far-Field, Long-Form Lectures/Meetings (as of Oct 2026)

Scope: ElevenLabs Scribe, Speechmatics, AssemblyAI, Deepgram Nova-3, OpenAI gpt-4o-transcribe, Google Chirp, AWS Transcribe, Azure, Rev.ai, Gladia, Soniox, Mistral Voxtral, Whisper large-v3/turbo, Parakeet, Canary-Qwen. All dollar/WER figures dated; vendor-reported vs. independent flagged inline.

## Which datasets measure these conditions, and what are published WERs by provider/model?

### Takeaway
No single independent benchmark covers all 13 systems across accent + noise + far-field + long-form. The closest things to independent, reproducible ground truth are the Hugging Face Open ASR Leaderboard (open models, per-dataset including AMI far-field and Earnings-22 accented-call data) and academic corpora (EdAcc, Earnings-21/22, AMI, CHiME-8). Commercial-vendor comparisons mostly come from vendor-run or vendor-commissioned benchmarks (AssemblyAI/Coval, Deepgram, ElevenLabs, Artificial Analysis) that should be read as marketing-adjacent rather than peer-reviewed.

### Cited Findings
- **Open ASR Leaderboard** (arXiv:2510.06961, "reproducible and transparent multilingual ASR evaluation," Hugging Face, 2025) benchmarks 60+ open/proprietary systems across 11 datasets including AMI Meeting Corpus (far-field), Earnings-22 (accented, spontaneous), TED-LIUM v3 (long-form lecture), LibriSpeech and Common Voice — [Open ASR Leaderboard paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961v3)
- Average English WER (leaderboard, independent, as queried Oct 2026): NVIDIA Canary-Qwen 2.5B 5.63%; IBM Granite Speech 3.3 8B 5.74%; NVIDIA Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 6.32% (Parakeet TDT 0.6B v2 reported elsewhere at 6.05% WER, RTFx 3386); OpenAI Whisper large-v3 7.44% (rank ~25 of 60+) — [Open ASR Leaderboard](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06961v3), [Northflank 2026 benchmark roundup](https://northflank.com/blog/best-open-source-speech-to-text-stt-model-in-2026-benchmarks)
- Leaderboard churn in 2026: Cohere's Transcribe (2B, Apache 2.0) took #1 in March 2026 at 5.42% average WER; IBM Granite Speech 4.1 2B took #1 five weeks later at 5.33% — [select.keenable.ai leaderboard history](https://select.keenable.ai/r/speech-recognition-accuracy-leaders-2022-2026/JbURQjlVooLQ6J10NkbRVw)
- On the **AMI-IHM** (close-mic) leaderboard specifically: Granite-speech-4.1-2b 8.09% WER (2025); Cohere-transcribe 8.13% (2026); Granite Speech 3.3 2B 8.90% (2025) — [Codesota AMI-IHM benchmark page](https://www.codesota.com/benchmark/ami-ihm). Note this is IHM (headset), not SDM (far-field) — far-field-specific per-system numbers were not retrievable from the pages fetched (see Gaps).
- Historical reference point for the IHM-vs-far-field gap on AMI: an RT05-era system scored 30.6% WER on IHM vs. 42.0% on MDM (multiple distant mic) — roughly an 11-point far-field penalty — [AMI Meeting STT System release notes](https://corpora.tika.apache.org/base/docs/govdocs1/916/916315.pdf) (2005 system, included only to illustrate the scale of the IHM/far-field gap historically, not current SOTA)
- **Earnings-21** (arXiv:2104.11348, Del Rio et al., Rev.com, Interspeech 2021, independent/academic): 39-hour corpus of entity-dense earnings-call speech; compared 4 commercial ASR systems + 2 internal + open-source Kaldi; Kaldi (open-source baseline) scored 48.8% WER, commercial systems scored meaningfully lower but exact per-vendor figures were not retrievable from available sources — [Earnings-21 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.11348)
- **Earnings-22** (arXiv:2203.15591, Del Rio et al., Rev.com, 2022, independent/academic): 125 files / 119 hours of earnings calls, speakers spanning 7 language regions and 27 countries (built specifically because "there is a lack of real-world, accented corpora to properly benchmark... models"); compared 4 commercial ASR systems via per-word Individual WER (IWER) broken out by country of origin, showing some accent features hurt some models much more than others — [Earnings-22 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.15591)
- **EdAcc — Edinburgh International Accents of English Corpus** (arXiv:2303.18110, Sanabria et al., University of Edinburgh, ICASSP 2023, independent/academic): ~40 hours of dyadic video-call conversations across diverse L1/L2 English varieties. Best model tested (trained on 680k hours — i.e., Whisper-scale) averaged 19.7% WER on EdAcc vs. 2.7% WER on clean US-English read speech — a ~17-point accent penalty. Performance dropped specifically for Indian, Jamaican, and Nigerian English speakers across all models tested — [EdAcc paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.18110)
- **AppTek Call-Center Dialogues** (arXiv:2604.27543, 2026): multi-accent, long-form, telephone-quality (8 kHz) call-center benchmark testing Canary-Qwen, Granite Speech, Kyutai Streaming ASR, Phi-4 Mini, Qwen3-ASR, and Whisper. Found accents en_SG (Singapore), en_CN, en_GB_SCT (Scottish), and en_IN consistently produced higher error rates across all systems tested, while en_AU and general US English produced the lowest WER. Exact per-accent numeric tables were not extractable from the fetched PDF (binary/compressed stream) — see Gaps — [AppTek Call-Center Dialogues](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.27543)
- **CHiME-8 DASR / NOTSOFAR-1** (arXiv:2407.16447, 2409.02041, 2507.18161; CHiME 2024 challenge, independent/academic): far-field, multi-channel, array-agnostic meeting ASR+diarization challenge. USTC-NERCSLIP's submitted system scored a tcpWER (time-constrained minimum-permutation WER, a joint ASR+diarization metric) of 12.87% (Stage 1, dev set) and 12.83% (Stage 3, dev set) on the NOTSOFAR-1 track. The CHiME-7/8 review paper notes that accurate total speaker counting is the single most challenging component and the largest source of downstream recognition error in distant meeting ASR — [CHiME-8 DASR challenge paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.16447), [CHiME-7/8 review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.18161), [USTC-NERCSLIP NOTSOFAR-1 system](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.02041)
- **AssemblyAI 26-dataset comparison** (vendor-reported, published March 2026): average normalized WER across 26 real-world datasets — AssemblyAI Universal-3 Pro 7.18%; ElevenLabs Scribe v2 7.65%; Azure Batch 8.56%; OpenAI GPT-4o Transcribe 8.94%; Deepgram Nova-3 9.03% — [AssemblyAI benchmarks](https://www.assemblyai.com/explore/stt-evals-hub)
- **AssemblyAI / Coval benchmark** (vendor-hosted, third-party Coval dataset, rebuilt July 2026): 3,500-sample set across six real-world conditions (clipping, far-field mics, phone-codec reverb, accents, noise). Overall WER: AssemblyAI Universal-3.5 Pro 3.2–3.4% (two AssemblyAI pages give slightly different headline numbers); Google Chirp 3 4.3%; Speechmatics Enhanced 4.3%; Cartesia Ink 2 4.6%; xAI Grok STT 4.7%; ElevenLabs Scribe v2 5.4%; Soniox STT RT v5 5.8%; Deepgram Nova-3 6%. AssemblyAI states Universal-3.5 Pro specifically led on clipping and far-field audio, and scored 5.62% WER on accented English specifically. Condition-by-condition numbers (i.e., WER just for the far-field slice, just for the accent slice) were not retrievable from the pages fetched — only overall and "which vendor led on which condition" — [AssemblyAI voice-agent-accuracy report](https://www.assemblyai.com/reports/voice-agent-accuracy)
- **Deepgram Nova-3** (vendor-reported, Feb 2025 press release): 5.26% WER batch, 6.84% WER streaming; vendor claims 47.4% (batch) and 54.2% (streaming) error-rate reduction vs. "next-best competitor." Nova-3 Medical: 3.45% median WER — [Deepgram Nova-3 announcement](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250212973938/en)
- **Independent counter-read on Deepgram**: Artificial Analysis's WER Index measures Nova-3 at roughly 7–10% on real-world recordings — notably higher than Deepgram's own 5.26% claim, illustrating the vendor-vs-independent gap common across this market — [Artificial Analysis / vexascribe summary](https://vexascribe.com/how-accurate-is-deepgram)
- **ElevenLabs Scribe v2**: Superwhisper benchmark (semi-independent third-party) gives 7.5% WER macro-average over 8 datasets; Artificial Analysis AA-WER (semi-independent) gives 2.2%; on FLEURS, vendor claims 93.5% accuracy (~6.5% WER) for Scribe v2 Realtime, the lowest WER of low-latency models tested, and vendor claims beating Google Gemini and Whisper on specific languages (e.g., Amharic 3.1% WER on FLEURS, 5.5% on Common Voice) — [Superwhisper ElevenLabs benchmark](https://superwhisper.com/benchmarks/elevenlabs-scribe-v2), [Artificial Analysis Scribe v2 page](https://artificialanalysis.ai/speech-to-text/models/elevenlabs-scribe-v2)
- **Artificial Analysis AA-WER v2 Index** (semi-independent benchmark site; methodology: duration-weighted average WER across ~8 hours spanning AA-AgentTalk 50%, VoxPopuli-Cleaned 25%, Earnings22-Cleaned 25%): mid-2026 leaders — UNFun-Realtime-ASR-preview 1.7%; ElevenLabs Scribe v2 2.2%; MAI Transcribe 1.5 2.4% — [Artificial Analysis STT leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/speech-to-text/non-streaming)
- **Mistral Voxtral** (vendor-reported, released July 2025, Apache-2.0): Voxtral Small 24B 2.1% WER English / 3.8% multilingual; Voxtral Mini 3B 3.2% / 4.9%. Same source's comparison figures: GPT-4o Audio 2.8%/4.1%; Whisper large-v3 2.4%/3.9% — [Mistral Voxtral benchmark writeup](https://whispernotes.app/blog/introducing-mistral-voxtral-models)
- **OpenAI gpt-4o-transcribe**: vendor claims it outperforms Whisper v2/v3 on the FLEURS multilingual benchmark due to "targeted reinforcement learning and midtraining with diverse, high-quality audio," with fewer misrecognitions specifically in "heavy accents, noisy rooms, and varying speech speeds" — no independently-verified or exact WER numbers for this claim were found (see Gaps) — [OpenAI community thread attempting FLEURS reproduction](https://community.openai.com/t/reproducing-gpt-4o-transcribe-fleurs-results/1151187)
- **Google Chirp / Chirp 2 / Chirp 3**: vendor documentation describes Chirp 2 improvements (multilingual accuracy, word-level timestamps, model adaptation, speech translation) but no independent WER table was found for Chirp 2 specifically; Chirp 3 scored 4.3% WER on the AssemblyAI/Coval real-world benchmark (second-party, see above) — [Google Chirp 2 docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/speech-to-text/docs/models/chirp-2)
- **AWS Transcribe vs. Azure** (2025 academic study on multilingual meetings — English, Thai, Malay): English reached up to 90.4% transcription accuracy (~9.6% WER) across the three cloud providers tested (Google, AWS, Azure); Azure and Amazon outperformed Google specifically on Thai; Azure Batch Transcription was most cost-effective at $0.18/hour with 76.4% mean accuracy (that 76.4% figure appears to be averaged across all three languages tested, not English alone — flagged as ambiguous) — [Benchmarking Cloud STT APIs for Multilingual Meetings](https://kirim.kmutt.ac.th/converis/portal/detail/Publication/1533270143) (academic, independent)
- General industry commentary (lower-confidence, blog-level, not a controlled study) holds that Azure is "well ahead" of AWS Transcribe on accuracy, and that AWS Transcribe's pipeline is slower because audio must first be uploaded to S3 before transcription — [fish.audio STT comparison blog](https://fish.audio/blog/best-speech-to-text-tools), 2025

### Inferences
- The accent and far-field penalty is structural, not vendor-specific: every independent academic corpus (EdAcc, Earnings-22, AMI SDM/MDM, AppTek Call-Center) shows a 2–4x (or worse) WER increase moving from clean/native/close-mic speech to accented/far-field/telephone speech, across every system tested, including the newest 2026 models.
- Vendor self-reported numbers (Deepgram, AssemblyAI, ElevenLabs) consistently show each vendor "winning" on its own benchmark; the only two genuinely third-party cross-vendor comparison points found (Artificial Analysis, AssemblyAI's Coval-hosted benchmark) rank vendors differently from each other and from each vendor's own press materials, so any cost/accuracy tradeoff analysis should weight independent benchmarks (Open ASR Leaderboard, Earnings-21/22, EdAcc, academic cloud-API comparison) over vendor press releases.
- For the macOS lecture-recording use case specifically, the AMI SDM/MDM gap and the AppTek/EdAcc accent findings together suggest the worst-case scenario (back-of-room mic + French-accented lecturer) should be modeled as compounding, not additive, error — no study found directly measures the combined far-field+accent condition, which is a genuine gap (see below).

### Gaps
- No study was found that tests the *combination* of far-field capture AND non-native accent in the same recording (i.e., a back-of-the-classroom mic plus a French-accented lecturer) — all datasets found isolate one variable (accent OR distance OR noise), not the realistic compound case.
- Condition-specific (not just overall) WER breakdowns for the AssemblyAI/Coval benchmark (i.e., the far-field-only number, the accent-only number, per vendor) could not be extracted from the fetched pages; only overall scores and "who led on which condition" qualitative statements were available.
- No independent, controlled, same-audio comparison across all 13 named systems (ElevenLabs, Speechmatics, AssemblyAI, Deepgram, gpt-4o-transcribe, Chirp, AWS, Azure, Rev.ai, Gladia, Soniox, Voxtral, Whisper/Parakeet/Canary) exists in one place; every source compares a subset.
- Exact per-vendor WER numbers for Earnings-21 and Earnings-22 (not just "Kaldi got 48.8%, commercial beat it") were not retrievable from the sources fetched — the papers likely contain tables not extracted via the automated fetch.

## Long-form failure modes: hallucinations, dropped segments, repetition loops — which providers/models show fewer, with evidence?

### Takeaway
Whisper's long-form hallucination and repetition-loop problem is well-documented and quantified in independent academic work (40.3% of inferences showed some hallucination, 9.1% showed repetition looping specifically, in one large-scale study). No equivalent independent, controlled hallucination-rate study was found for any of the commercial APIs (AssemblyAI, Deepgram, ElevenLabs, Speechmatics, etc.) or for Parakeet/Canary-Qwen — this is the single largest evidence gap in this research task.

### Cited Findings
- **"Investigation of Whisper ASR Hallucinations"** (arXiv:2501.11378, Jan 2025, independent/academic): across over 301,000 Whisper inferences, hallucinations appeared in 40.3% of cases, with 9.1% of all cases specifically involving repetition/"looping" — the most concrete quantified number found for this failure mode — [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.11378v1)
- **WhisperX** (arXiv:2303.00747, independent/academic): demonstrates that Whisper's native approach (relying on its own decoded timestamp tokens to chunk long audio) is itself a hallucination/repetition-loop trigger; WhisperX instead uses external VAD (voice activity detection) to segment audio before transcribing each segment, which the paper shows reports the lowest insertion-error rate of methods compared and avoids repetitive transcription loops and hallucinating speech during silent/inactive regions — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.00747)
- **Mechanistic interpretability study** (arXiv:2508.15882, Aug 2025, independent/academic): identifies the specific triggers for repetition hallucinations — repetitive content in the input audio itself, code-switching between languages mid-utterance, and fragmented/heavily-noised/unclear audio input — all three are plausible in lecture recordings (repeated phrases, French-English code-switching, HVAC/projector noise) — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.15882)
- **"Whisper-CD"** (arXiv:2603.06193, 2026, independent/academic): confirms the hallucination/repetition-loop/content-omission problem persists in long-form Whisper transcription as of 2026 and proposes a mitigation, implying the base-model problem was not solved by large-v3/turbo alone — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.06193)
- The original Whisper paper (arXiv:2212.04356, OpenAI, 2022 — vendor/original-source, peer-reviewed-adjacent) reports Whisper large-v2 at 3.5% WER on the long-form TED-LIUM3 validation set using 30-second chunking — a useful baseline long-form lecture number, though it predates large-v3/turbo — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.04356)

### Inferences
- Because WhisperX-style external VAD segmentation measurably reduces Whisper's hallucination/repetition behavior, any product built on Whisper (open-source stack) for lecture transcription should pre-segment audio with VAD rather than rely on Whisper's native long-form decoding — this is an actionable mitigation even without switching vendors.
- The lack of any found independent hallucination-rate study for commercial APIs (Deepgram, AssemblyAI, Speechmatics, etc.) is itself informative: it suggests hallucination is treated by the industry as a "Whisper problem" specific to the sequence-to-sequence decoder-with-timestamp-tokens architecture, and that streaming/CTC-style commercial systems (Deepgram, Speechmatics) may be structurally less prone to this failure mode — but this is an inference, not a measured fact.

### Gaps
- No quantified hallucination or repetition-loop rate was found for AssemblyAI, Deepgram, Speechmatics, ElevenLabs Scribe, Rev.ai, Gladia, Soniox, Google Chirp, AWS Transcribe, Azure, gpt-4o-transcribe, Voxtral, Parakeet, or Canary-Qwen specifically. The Canary-Qwen/Parakeet noise-robustness numbers found (see next section) measure WER degradation, not hallucination/looping incidence, which is a distinct failure mode.
- No study was found that measures "dropped segments" (silent omission of entire spoken passages) as a distinct metric from WER for any provider.

## Speechmatics and others publish accent/demographic fairness results — what numbers, and are any independently replicated?

### Takeaway
Speechmatics' flagship fairness claims are vendor-reported and from 2020 (likely outdated given five years of model iteration since); more recent independent academic fairness benchmarks exist (Fair-Speech/ASR-FAIRBENCH, RW-Voice-EQ Bench, EdAcc) but none of them specifically re-test or replicate Speechmatics' 2020 numbers.

### Cited Findings
- **Speechmatics "Global English" whitepaper** (vendor-reported, Sept 2020): accent-agnostic English model trained on data from 40+ countries. Vendor claims: 3%–55% more accurate than Google Cloud Speech API's accent-specific language packs; 5%–23% more accurate than IBM Watson's US English pack; ~92% accuracy on children's speech vs. ~83% for Google and Deepgram (as tested in 2020); test set was ~4 hours of diverse audio varying gender, age, and region — [Speechmatics Global English whitepaper, Sept 2020](https://www.speechmatics.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Global-English-Accent-Independent-Speech-Recognition-Whitepaper-September-2020.pdf). **This is six years old as of Oct 2026 and vendor-authored; no independent replication of these specific percentages was found.**
- **ASR-FAIRBENCH** (independent/academic framework, exact paper not isolated but referenced across multiple 2025/2026 sources): uses Meta's Fair-Speech dataset (~26,470 utterances, all US English speakers reading identical prompted sentences, which removes vocabulary as a confound so WER differences are attributable only to acoustic/model factors); computes a Fairness Adjusted ASR Score (FAAS) via mixed-effects Poisson regression combining WER and demographic-group equity — exact FAAS numbers per vendor were not retrievable from the sources found (see Gaps)
- **RW-Voice-EQ Bench** (arXiv:2607.14846, 2026, independent/academic): finds that native-vs-non-native speaker status is a more informative fairness axis than regional-accent labels, and measures a 1.5–2x WER gap for second-language English speakers vs. native speakers, attributing this to "Inner-Circle" (US/UK/Australia-centric) bias in training data — directly relevant to a French-accented-lecturer use case — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.14846)
- **"Towards Fair ASR For Second Language Speakers Using Fairness Prompted Finetuning"** (arXiv:2510.18374, Oct 2025, independent/academic): proposes evaluation via per-accent-group WER, macro-averaged WER across groups, and a min-max gap metric (worst-performing group vs. best-performing group) as the standard fairness-reporting trio — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.18374)
- **EdAcc** (see above, arXiv:2303.18110): already functions as an independent, replicated-style fairness check in that multiple models were tested on the same corpus and all showed the same qualitative pattern — worse performance on Indian, Jamaican, and Nigerian English specifically — [paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.18110)
- **AppTek Call-Center Dialogues** (arXiv:2604.27543, 2026) independently corroborates the qualitative pattern that certain accents (en_SG, en_CN, en_GB_SCT, en_IN) consistently underperform en_AU/en_US across multiple different modern systems (Canary-Qwen, Granite Speech, Whisper, Qwen3-ASR, etc.) — this is the closest thing to "independent replication" of the general finding that non-US/UK accents are systematically disadvantaged, even though it doesn't replicate Speechmatics' specific numbers — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.27543)

### Inferences
- The consistent, cross-study qualitative finding (EdAcc 2023, AppTek 2026, RW-Voice-EQ 2026) that non-native/non-Western-English accents underperform by a wide margin regardless of which specific model is tested gives reasonably high confidence that French-accented lecturer audio will show meaningfully higher WER than native-English lecturer audio on literally any of the 13 systems in scope — even though no single study tests French-accented English specifically against all 13 vendors.
- Speechmatics' specific fairness-whitepaper numbers (2020) should not be relied upon for an Oct-2026 product decision; they are both vendor-authored and stale relative to five years of subsequent model updates from Speechmatics and all competitors.

### Gaps
- No study specifically measuring French-accented English WER was found for any of the 13 named commercial systems; the closest proxy evidence is (a) a small academic pilot study on French learners of English transcribed by various Whisper model sizes (found but not deeply extracted — see below), and (b) the general non-native-speaker WER gap (1.5–2x) from RW-Voice-EQ Bench.
- No current (2024–2026) Speechmatics-published fairness/demographic benchmark was found — only the 2020 Global English whitepaper. It's unclear whether Speechmatics has published an updated fairness report since; this should be checked directly against speechmatics.com if a more current number is needed.
- The French-learners-of-English Whisper pilot study (found via search, not independently fetched for exact numbers) reportedly found that medium-sized Whisper models converge with native-speaker intelligibility judgments while the tiny model produces errors resembling plausible learner mistakes — but exact WER figures were not extracted; a follow-up fetch of that specific paper would be needed for citable numbers.

## Telephone/VoIP call audio (8 kHz, compressed) accuracy evidence

### Takeaway
There is a well-established, if loosely quantified, rule of thumb that narrowband telephony (8 kHz, G.711-codec) roughly doubles-to-triples WER versus wideband (16 kHz) audio, and VoIP-specific degradations (packet loss, jitter, reverb) stack on top of that. The one benchmark purpose-built for this (AppTek Call-Center Dialogues) is recent (2026) but its exact numbers weren't extractable from the fetched PDF.

### Cited Findings
- General industry guidance (gnani.ai, vendor-ish/explainer-level source, 2026): "a system that achieves 5% WER on 16kHz audio may show 12 to 18% WER on the same content at 8kHz" — [Speech-to-Text glossary, gnani.ai, June 2026](https://www.gnani.ai/resources/blogs/blog-speech-to-text-glossary-asr-terminology)
- Standard telephony is 8 kHz / 8-bit / 64 kbit/s using the G.711 codec; G.711 compresses exactly the frequency information most useful for distinguishing similar-sounding words, and live VoIP calls additionally introduce packet loss and jitter not present in demo/test conditions (demos often run over 48kHz WebRTC while production runs over 8kHz PSTN/G.711 trunks) — [Soniox: Audio formats for speech recognition](https://soniox.com/wiki/audio-formats-speech-recognition)
- Speech-tuned lossy codecs (e.g., Opus) at a reasonable bitrate are "nearly free" in terms of accuracy loss, in contrast to generic lossy codecs (MP3/AAC) or aggressive VoIP compression, which does measurably hurt accuracy — [Soniox: Audio formats for speech recognition](https://soniox.com/wiki/audio-formats-speech-recognition)
- **AppTek Call-Center Dialogues** (arXiv:2604.27543, 2026) is explicitly built as a telephone-quality (8 kHz), multi-accent, long-form call-center benchmark testing Canary-Qwen, Granite Speech, Kyutai Streaming ASR, Phi-4 Mini, Qwen3-ASR, and Whisper — designed precisely to close the gap between clean-read-speech benchmarks (LibriSpeech, Common Voice) and real deployed telephony conditions. Exact WER tables were not extractable from the fetched PDF (binary/compressed stream issue) — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.27543)
- A related but narrower academic study, "Multi-style Training for South African Call Centre Audio" (arXiv:2202.07219), addresses the same telephony/call-center domain, indicating sustained academic interest in this specific condition since at least 2022 — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.07219)
- The AssemblyAI/Coval real-world benchmark (vendor-hosted, 2026, see above) explicitly includes "phone-codec reverb" as one of its six test conditions, and states AssemblyAI Universal-3.5 Pro "topped the field... on far-field audio" — implying it was also evaluated (though not reported with a standalone number) on phone-codec audio — [AssemblyAI voice-agent-accuracy report](https://www.assemblyai.com/reports/voice-agent-accuracy)

### Inferences
- For the VC founder-call use case (Teams/Zoom, not raw PSTN), the relevant risk is lower than classic 8kHz telephone risk as long as calls stay on native WebRTC/Teams audio pipelines (typically 16kHz+ wideband) — the 8kHz G.711 penalty mainly applies if a call is bridged through a traditional phone dial-in number rather than the native app client.
- Because no exact per-vendor 8kHz WER table was retrievable, a product decision that depends heavily on telephone-quality accuracy should either directly test candidate vendors against sample 8kHz audio, or request vendor-specific telephony benchmark numbers directly (several vendors, e.g., AssemblyAI and Rev.ai, have historically published phone-call-specific comparison blog posts — see Gaps).

### Gaps
- Exact numeric WER results from the AppTek Call-Center Dialogues paper (per-system, per-accent, 8kHz) could not be extracted — the PDF fetch returned only compressed binary/metadata, not table contents. A follow-up with a PDF-specific extraction tool or direct paper read would be needed.
- A specifically relevant-looking source, "Comparing Speech-to-Text APIs on Phone Call Transcription" (AssemblyAI blog, webflow.assemblyai.com), was found in search results but not fetched for its actual content/numbers — this should be retrieved directly if phone-call-specific vendor comparison numbers are needed.
- No specific WER numbers were found for Rev.ai, Gladia, or Soniox on telephone/VoIP audio specifically (as distinct from their general real-world benchmark numbers cited above).

## Studies of diarization quality for 2-4 speaker calls (useful for founder calls)

### Takeaway
A genuinely independent, multi-vendor diarization benchmark exists (pyannote.ai's 2026 benchmark, 259 recordings / ~67 hours across 10 domains including conversational telephone speech) and compares AssemblyAI, Deepgram, ElevenLabs, Soniox, and Speechmatics alongside pyannote's own models — but exact DER numbers could not be extracted from the page fetched. Scattered vendor-reported DER figures (Fireflies.ai, pyannoteAI, DiariZen) exist but are not a controlled apples-to-apples comparison, and a secondary-source caveat holds that real meetings with natural speaker overlap show DER well above the cooperative-audio numbers vendors publish.

### Cited Findings
- **pyannote.ai benchmark** (2026, run by a diarization vendor but testing competitors too — semi-independent): evaluates "10 distinct areas of use," 259 recordings, ~67 hours of "challenging, multi-domain audio," including DIHARD-style categories (meetings, courtroom speech, conversational telephone interactions); explicitly compares pyannoteAI (Precision-3, OSS Community-1) against AssemblyAI (Universal-Pro-3.5), Deepgram (Nova-3), ElevenLabs, Soniox, and Speechmatics. Specific DER percentages per vendor/condition were not extractable from the fetched page — [pyannote.ai benchmark](https://www.pyannote.ai/benchmark) (see Gaps — recommend direct re-fetch or manual visit for the actual numbers)
- Vendor-reported DER figures found via secondary aggregator sites (lower confidence, not independently verified): Fireflies.ai reports 7.2% DER (92.8% accuracy) on 2–4 speaker audio; pyannoteAI reports 11.2% DER overall; DiariZen (open-source) reports 13.3% DER — [kili-technology diarization guide, 2026](https://kili-technology.com/blog/speaker-diarization-models-guide-benchmarks-and-failure-modes-2026)
- Academic finding: DER increases monotonically with speaker count, with a clear, measurable step-up in error between 2- and 3-speaker sessions, and again between 3- and 4-speaker sessions, in meeting-style datasets like AMI/AliMeeting — [Benchmarking Diarization Models, arXiv:2509.26177](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.26177v1)
- **CHiME-8 DASR / NOTSOFAR-1** (see above, independent/academic, arXiv:2407.16447 and arXiv:2507.18161): confirms that in realistic far-field, multi-speaker meeting audio, speaker counting error is the dominant source of downstream transcription error (not just a diarization-only metric problem) — directly relevant to multi-speaker founder calls recorded via a laptop mic rather than individual headsets
- **DIHARD II** (arXiv:1906.07839, 2019, independent/academic): foundational, harder-condition diarization challenge/dataset still used as a component category in newer benchmarks (e.g., pyannote.ai's 2026 benchmark references DIHARD-style categories) — useful as a difficulty baseline even though dated
- A secondary-source editorial caveat worth flagging rather than treating as hard data: "vendor-published DERs measure cooperative 2-4 speaker audio, but real meetings with overlap routinely show 20%+ DER regardless of tool" — this is a synthesized claim from a non-primary aggregator source, not a controlled study result, and should be weighted accordingly — [kili-technology diarization guide, 2026](https://kili-technology.com/blog/speaker-diarization-models-guide-benchmarks-and-failure-modes-2026)

### Inferences
- For 2–4 speaker VC founder calls over Teams/Zoom (not heavily overlapping, mostly turn-taking), diarization quality is likely to land well below the worst-case meeting-overlap numbers (20%+ DER) and closer to the vendor-claimed 7–13% DER range, since founder calls typically have less cross-talk than open-floor meetings — but this is an inference, not a directly measured result for this exact scenario.
- The repeated finding (CHiME-8 review, diarization-count-vs-speakers study) that speaker *counting* error — not just confusion between already-detected speakers — is the dominant failure mode suggests that for a product like this, correctly detecting "how many distinct speakers are on this call" matters more than fine-grained voice-matching accuracy once speaker count is known.
