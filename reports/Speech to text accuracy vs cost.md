# Beating Whisper Costs Little Or Nothing

Yapperroni's local Whisper large-v3-turbo (q5, 8.6% average WER on its 3-lecture benchmark, 13.4% on 26 unseen MIT OpenCourseWare lectures) sits in a specific, findable place on independent leaderboards: roughly tied with the market on clean, native-accent long-form speech, but a clear step behind both specialist cloud APIs and newer open-weight local models once audio gets muffled or accented — exactly Yapperroni's hardest cases. On the Hugging Face Open ASR Leaderboard's standardized harness, NVIDIA Canary-Qwen-2.5B cuts turbo's lecture-style (TED-LIUM) error by **24% relative** and IBM's tiny Granite Speech 4.0-1B cuts turbo's far-field-meeting (AMI) error by **48% relative** — both free, local, and Apache/CC-BY licensed ([Open ASR Leaderboard, live CSV, Oct 8 2026](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/open_asr_leaderboard/main/scripts/data/en_shortform.csv)). On the cloud side, AssemblyAI's Universal-3 Pro and ElevenLabs Scribe v2 run at roughly **half to a third of turbo's error rate** on Artificial Analysis's independent cross-vendor board, for about **$0.21–$0.22 per audio-hour batch** — cheap enough that testing every serious candidate against Yapperroni's own 29-lecture set costs under $15 in API fees, and $0 if new signup credits are used ([Artificial Analysis, non-streaming leaderboard, Oct 2026](https://artificialanalysis.ai/speech-to-text/non-streaming)). The one place nothing beats Whisper decisively is real-world, non-lab long-form audio: an independent academic study (PennSound) found turbo essentially tied with the best commercial system tested and ahead of four others, while also independently confirming the exact hallucination pattern Yapperroni found itself — full large-v3 "drops speech" under default decoding. The honest shape of the opportunity, then, is not "replace Whisper," it's "add one cheap or free specialist for the two conditions — far-field and accented speech — where turbo demonstrably falls over," and the cheapest of those fixes may already be sitting un-tuned inside Yapperroni's own Sherpa.swift pipeline.

## Turbo is mid-pack on leaderboards built for different audio than lectures

Every independent benchmark anchors Whisper differently because every benchmark weighs different audio. On the Hugging Face Open ASR Leaderboard — the only source in this research that live-pulls a standardized harness across both cloud-adjacent and fully open models — Whisper large-v3-turbo scores **7.83% average WER**, with **3.57% on TED-LIUM** (its best lecture-style proxy) and **16.13% on AMI** (its far-field-meeting proxy), while full large-v3 scores 7.44% average, 3.86% TED-LIUM, and 15.95% AMI ([HF Open ASR Leaderboard CSV, live-pulled Oct 8 2026](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/open_asr_leaderboard/main/scripts/data/en_shortform.csv); [leaderboard paper, arXiv:2510.06961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06961)). On Artificial Analysis's agent-call-weighted board, by contrast, large-v3 scores a much lower **4.1% AA-WER** — turbo isn't broken out as a distinct row there, a genuine gap in that leaderboard — while the top commercial systems (ElevenLabs Scribe v2, Microsoft MAI-Transcribe-2) sit near 2.0–2.2%, making Whisper look roughly **1.9–2x worse** than the leader ([Artificial Analysis, Oct 2026](https://artificialanalysis.ai/speech-to-text/non-streaming)). On the one genuinely independent academic benchmark that used real, non-lab long-form audio rather than curated clips — the April 2025 PennSound study, which ran AWS, Azure, Google, IBM, NVIDIA NeMo, Rev.ai, and Whisper over the same ~10 hours of recorded speech and scored them with NIST's SCTK toolkit — turbo (run with anti-hallucination decoding flags) scored **9.5% WER**, nearly tied with the best system tested (Rev.ai, 9.0%) and ahead of AWS (9.8%), Azure (10.2%), NeMo (10.9%), and Google (11.0%) ([Wright et al., arXiv:2504.05702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05702)). The honest multiplier for "how much worse is Whisper than the best alternative" therefore ranges from **roughly 1.0x on real long-form speech to ~1.4x on far-field/noisy benchmarks to ~2x on voice-agent-style short utterances** — there is no single number, and a report that gave one would be misleading.

Mapped onto Yapperroni's own numbers, this context matters. The 9.6% WER on the 26-lecture clean subset sits between HF's TED-LIUM number (3.57%) and its AMI number (16.13%) — plausible, since real MIT OCW audio is neither studio-clean read speech nor a four-person overlapping meeting. The 17.1% average on muffled lectures tracks close to HF's AMI figure, suggesting Yapperroni's "muffled" condition is genuinely AMI-grade far-field difficulty, not a mild degradation. One caveat worth flagging before taking any of these comparisons too literally: Yapperroni's scoring normalizer explicitly ignores case, punctuation, fillers, and numbers, while the standard Whisper/leaderboard normalizers used by HF and Artificial Analysis do not discard fillers the same way — Yapperroni's own WER numbers likely run a little optimistic relative to the leaderboards they're being compared against, which means the true gap to the cloud and local leaders is probably slightly larger than a naive side-by-side suggests, not smaller.

The most actionable finding in this entire research pass, though, is a contradiction inside Yapperroni's own data. On the live HF CSV, NVIDIA's Parakeet TDT v3 **beats** turbo on every relevant axis — average WER (6.32% vs 7.83%), TED-LIUM (2.80% vs 3.57%), AMI (11.39% vs 16.13%), and even accented speech via Earnings-22 (11.19% vs 11.63%, essentially tied). Yet in Yapperroni's own 3-lecture harness, Parakeet v3 scored **14.9%** — nearly 6 points *worse* than turbo's 8.6%, a complete inversion of the independent result. Because the independent data shows Parakeet matching turbo on accented audio specifically, accent alone can't explain the gap; the more likely explanations are a sherpa-onnx export or quantization difference, a windowing/chunking cut landing badly in `Sherpa.swift`'s long-audio logic, a sample-rate mismatch, or simply a 3-lecture mean dominated by one hard file. This is worth resolving before spending money on any cloud vendor, because if it's a pipeline bug rather than a model-quality gap, fixing it is a free, permanent accuracy win already sitting in code that ships today.

## Consumer dictation apps mostly wrap the same vendors, at a markup

For context on what the market already charges for "good enough" accuracy, the consumer dictation and meeting-notetaker segment splits into three camps. A few companies have funded genuinely proprietary acoustic models: **Wispr Flow** moved off wrapping OpenAI Whisper to a 2B-parameter in-house model ("Canto") in August 2026, claiming a cut from >30% to 5–10% WER in noisy/accented conditions (vendor claim, partially corroborated by a semi-independent competitor benchmark putting "Wispr Flow raw" at 8.7% blended WER) — priced at $12–15/month ([vktr.com](https://www.vktr.com/ai-platforms/wispr-hits-2b-valuation-launches-canto-voice-model/); [Superwhisper benchmark](https://superwhisper.com/benchmarks/wispr-flow-raw)). **Otter.ai** built its own in-house ASR and, in the one rigorously independent live-captioning test found (Disability World, NIST sclite scoring against a certified CART-writer control), ranked best of six live-captioning vendors at 6.2% WER — but that same test found WER spiking to **42.6%** for *every* vendor tested, Otter included, once 2–3 speakers overlapped, which is precisely the geometry of a VC founder call ([disabilityworld.org](https://www.disabilityworld.org/articles/live-caption-accuracy-benchmark/)). A second camp runs local, user-controlled Whisper or Parakeet with no subscription at all — **MacWhisper** ($69–$149 one-time), **VoiceInk** (open-source, $39.99 one-time, BYOK for Groq/OpenAI/Deepgram/ElevenLabs/Speechmatics/Soniox among others), and **Superwhisper** ($8.49/mo, $84.99/yr, or $249.99 lifetime) — the clearest architectural precedent for Yapperroni's own local-first design, proof that a zero-marginal-cost local engine can sustain a real business. The third camp — **Granola**, **Willow Voice**, **Fireflies**, **tl;dv**, **Fathom**, **Cluely** — declines to disclose its transcription vendor at all, treating STT as a commodity backend and marketing the LLM summarization layer instead; several of these monetize "unlimited transcription, metered AI credits," which independently confirms that raw transcription has become a loss-leader rather than a differentiator in this segment.

## Accents and far rooms compound, they don't just add

Every independent academic source in this research agrees on one structural point: accent penalty and distance penalty are not small, and they interact. The Edinburgh Accents of English Corpus (EdAcc) found a Whisper-scale model's WER jump from 2.7% on clean US-English read speech to 19.7% on diverse-accent dyadic calls — a 17-point penalty from accent alone ([arXiv:2303.18110](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.18110)). The RW-Voice-EQ Bench separately measured a 1.5–2x WER gap for second-language English speakers specifically, attributing it to "Inner-Circle" (US/UK/Australia-centric) training-data bias ([arXiv:2607.14846](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.14846)) — directly relevant to a French-accented lecturer. No academic study found tests the *combination* of far-field capture and non-native accent in a single recording, which is exactly the gap Yapperroni's own benchmark data fills with real numbers: on the 3-lecture set, the French-accented lecture went from **7.6% WER clean to 25.3% muffled — a 3.3x jump** — while the biology lecture went from 2.8% to 5.4% (1.9x) and the math lecture barely moved (5.1% to 5.2%). That gap between a roughly 2x degradation for noise alone and a 3.3x degradation for noise-plus-accent is itself evidence that these two failure modes compound multiplicatively rather than add, consistent with (and a rare real-world instance of) exactly the interaction the academic literature could only describe qualitatively. On the hallucination side, the independent Whisper-hallucination study found hallucinations in 40.3% of 301,000 inferences, with 9.1% specifically involving repetition loops ([arXiv:2501.11378](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.11378v1)), and PennSound's Table 3 ablation showed full large-v3 generating 124 hallucinated trailing tokens under default decoding — both independently corroborating Yapperroni's own finding that full large-v3 "drops speech on muffled audio" and scored a disqualifying 29.5% WER on the same 3-lecture set where turbo scored 8.6%.

## Conclusion

Three candidates are worth testing against Yapperroni's own 29-lecture set, each picked for a different constraint rather than for raw WER alone. **AssemblyAI Universal-3(.5) Pro** is the cloud pick: it is the only vendor in this research with independent evidence of leading specifically on far-field and accented audio (the Coval benchmark), it offers a genuine zero-retention opt-out with a dedicated EU endpoint — the privacy bar a VC-call use case needs before any cloud path is defensible — and AssemblyAI's own $50 signup credit covers the test outright. **Apple SpeechAnalyzer** is the zero-cost native pick: it's already on the hardware (macOS 26, M5 Air), requires no model download, runs natively streaming, and closes the single largest gap in this entire research pass — no one has ever benchmarked it against large-v3-turbo. **A per-lecture re-run of Parakeet TDT v3 inside the existing `Sherpa.swift` pipeline** is the free-lunch pick: the model independently beats turbo on every relevant metric, but Yapperroni's own harness shows the opposite, which points to a fixable pipeline issue rather than a model-quality gap — and if it's a bug, the fix is permanent and costs nothing.

Assuming MIT OpenCourseWare's typical ~50-minute lecture length, 29 lectures is roughly **24 hours of audio** (use actual-hours × vendor-$/hr to rescale if real lengths differ); the 3-lecture pilot that maps directly onto Yapperroni's existing 8.6%/10.6%/10.7%/13.7%/14.9%/29.5% rows is about 2.5 hours. Running the full 29-lecture set through AssemblyAI costs roughly $5 (async) to $11 (streaming) at list price — covered entirely by the $50 free credit, making the actual out-of-pocket cost **$0**. The SpeechAnalyzer and Parakeet tests have no API cost at all; their cost is engineering time, not dollars — a bridge to `AVFoundation`'s `SpeechAnalyzer`/`SpeechTranscriber` APIs for the former, and adding per-lecture (not just per-set) logging to the existing sherpa integration for the latter. The cheapest possible next step, in other words, may not be signing up for a new vendor at all — it's finding out why a model that already beats turbo on paper doesn't beat it in Yapperroni's own code.
